Most of what we read on the web is set by default. The browser picks a size, a line height, and a measure, and the words land wherever they land. It works, mostly, but it rarely feels considered.

A page that is meant to be read deserves a little more care. Not ornament, just attention: a comfortable line length, enough space between lines for the eye to find its way back, and a clear sense of what matters most.

## Start with the measure

The measure is the length of a line of text. Too long and the reader loses their place on the return trip; too short and the rhythm turns choppy. Somewhere around sixty to seventy characters is a good place to begin, and the rest of the page can be built around it.

### A note on line height

Line height and measure work together. Longer lines want a little more leading so the eye can track back to the start of the next line. Shorter lines can afford to be set tighter.

### Hierarchy without noise

Headings should guide, not shout. A modest change in size or weight is usually enough to signal a new section, especially when the body text around it is calm and consistent.

## Then let the color recede

Text and background should sit comfortably together in both light and dark. Near-black on near-white, and the reverse, with nothing competing for attention. The words do the work.
